Physics & AI — How This Works

Understanding the science behind energy prediction — the connection between Physics formulas and Machine Learning models.

Physics Foundation

Ohm's Law & Power Formula
P = V × I   (Electrical Power in Watts)
  • P = Power (Watts, W)
  • V = Voltage (Volts, V) — the electrical pressure
  • I = Current (Amperes, A) — the flow of charge
Energy Equation
E = P × t / 1000   (Energy in kilowatt-hours)
  • E = Energy consumed (kWh — what you pay for)
  • t = Time (hours)
  • Division by 1000 converts Wh to kWh
Example Calculation

An appliance at 230V drawing 5A for 6 hours:

  • Power: P = 230 × 5 = 1,150W = 1.15 kW
  • Energy: E = 1.15 × 6 = 6.9 kWh
  • Daily Cost (at ₹6/kWh): 6.9 × 6 = ₹41.40

Machine Learning Approach

Why ML on Top of Physics?

The physics formula gives an ideal calculation. But real-world energy consumption is affected by:

  • Temperature: AC, heaters, and fans adjust consumption based on climate
  • Number of appliances: Interactions between devices (e.g., fridge + AC both drawing power)
  • Historical patterns: Habits, seasonal variations, and usage trends
  • Non-linear effects: Physics assumes constant voltage/current; reality is variable

ML captures these non-linear, multi-variable relationships that a simple formula cannot.

ML Pipeline
  1. Input Features: voltage, current, usage_hours, number_of_appliances, temperature, previous_consumption
  2. Feature Scaling: StandardScaler (zero mean, unit variance)
  3. Model Training: RandomForestRegressor + LinearRegression
  4. Model Selection: Best R² score → saved as energy_model.pkl
  5. Prediction: Scaled features → ML model → predicted kWh
Random Forest
  • Ensemble of decision trees
  • Captures non-linear patterns
  • Resistant to overfitting
  • Best for: complex feature interactions
Linear Regression
  • y = β₀ + β₁x₁ + ... + βₙxₙ
  • Interpretable coefficients
  • Baseline for comparison
  • Best for: linear relationships
